**Problem**

Rapid urban transformation and economic development in Seoul have resulted in a city compressed unevenly by modernization. Under a technologically advanced and culturally dynamic façade exist spaces characterized by rigid social expectations and stubbornly homogenized patterns of conformity. This contradiction can be understood through the forced coexistence of disparate social conditions that are altogether spurred by extraordinary urban progress.

This tension is especially evident in Seoul's leisure spaces. Cafés, shops, and entertainment venues function not as spaces for spontaneous social interaction, but programmed destinations for the consumption and propagation of trends. A particular architectural opportunity to study this phenomenon is the nightclub. Nightlife is historically rooted in cultural expression and experimentation, and thus offers a space to counter the stagnant conventions. However, nightclubs themselves are also subject to the commercialization and conformity that characterize other social spaces in Seoul.

**Relevance**

The complex tension between Seoul's development and culture can be read from a sociological standpoint, with sociologist Hagen Koo describing modernization as an ongoing negotiation between competing social and cultural forces (and not a linear progression from "old" to "new"). Kyung-Sup Chang similarly describes how rapid urban transformation across separate conditions produces the simultaneous existence of contradictory relationships. 

More specifically, recent commentary examines a manifestation of this tension in the realm of social media, where physical environments are increasingly designed around their photographic representation and digital circulation. While the relationship between these physical spaces and digital interactions introduce new possibilities for human relationships, they also raise questions about the authenticity of the choreographed experience and consumption they encourage. In fact, my own experiences in Seoul have led me to question whether the city's abundance of modern, creative architecture necessarily translates to actual expression and social interaction.

The conversation around nightclubs is relevant, as music events in Seoul are highly synonymous with their online promotion and trendification. However, the architectural history of nightclubs suggests a radical approach to counter this social phenomenon. In the 1960s and 1970s, radical architecture collectives such as Gruppo 9999 viewed the nightclub as a laboratory for spatial experimentation. By accommodating performances and installations, early proposals for nightclubs as experimental spaces represented new architecture, not just as a design typology but a flexible model for social organization. Rather than a designed space with a fixed program, the nightclub is an environment shaped by the participation of its occupants.

**Proposal**

My thesis will be an architectural design project, motivated by research on how nightclub architecture might encourage spontaneous social interaction and individual expression in contemporary Seoul. While I am motivated by the city's overall urban culture and sociological analysis, I intend to focus on the architectural scale of the nightclub, and design around spatial organization that can encourage social behavior within the space.

Specifically, I plan to place research emphasis on nightclubs of the past, such as Gruppo 9999's work, alongside comparisons of contemporary nightlife in various cities with distinct histories of urban development, including Seoul and Tokyo. Specific case studies will help identify architectural strategies and inform me on how they can facilitate authentic social experiences. The final deliverable for the project is a design proposal (including plans, sections, renderings, physical models, and other appropriate forms of visualization) for a nightclub in Seoul. Can the nightclub function according to an experimental architectural typology, challenging the prescriptive tension between commercial space and social behavior in Seoul?
